# North Texas QB Tayven Jackson out for regular season; Chris Jimerson Jr. takes over

Jackson is out for the next two games with an “upper body” injury. Jimerson will make first career start vs. Tulane.

by Steve Helwick

Nov 18, 2026, 4:30 AM UTC

North Texas lost every starter from the winningest team in program history. Yet, the Mean Green immediately reestablished themselves as one of the American Conference’s top contenders.

They entered Saturday night riding a six-game winning streak and stood as the last remaining undefeated team in the conference standings. But when North Texas fell 35-16 to UTSA in San Antonio, the Mean Green lost more than their unblemished conference record.

Starting quarterback Tayven Jackson exited the contest in the first minute of the second half after converting a third-and-long with an 18-yard scramble. Jackson took a hard fall on his right shoulder and did not return. He was later spotted wearing a sling on his right arm, with his shoulder wrapped.

Head coach Neal Brown confirmed his starting quarterback will miss the final two games of the regular season, although he didn’t rule out a return for a potential conference championship or bowl game.

“He’ll miss both of these games, and then we’ll know after that,” Brown said. “He’ll definitely miss Saturday and then the UAB game as well. I’m not ready to say he’s out for the season yet. I think after that will be to be determined.”

Jackson currently ranks sixth in the FBS with 3,005 passing yards and ninth with 23 passing touchdowns. The fifth-year senior, who previously started games for Indiana and UCF, captained an offense that ranked first in the country in yards per game prior to Saturday night’s loss at UTSA.

Brown didn’t specify if the injury was to the shoulder or collarbone, or if another part of the upper body was affected.

“It’s upper body,” Brown said. “I don’t believe it will inhibit his training for the NFL. I’m not ready to rule him out for the season. He will miss the final two regular season games. We will know more in the next week or so.”

North Texas was already dealing with a depleted quarterback room. Second-string quarterback Chaston Ditta, who transferred from East Carolina, suffered an injury in October and remains sidelined, although Brown did not rule him out for the season. That left sophomore Chris Jimerson Jr. as the next man up following Jackson’s exit against UTSA.

Upon checking in, Jimerson completed the touchdown drive that Jackson started. Then, he led a second scoring drive, cutting North Texas’ 21-3 deficit into 21-16. Trailing by five, the backup quarterback possessed the ball in UTSA territory with under five minutes remaining. However, the Roadrunner defense thwarted the Mean Green’s comeback effort with a critical fourth down sack, sealing North Texas’ first loss since Sept. 19.

“With limited reps, I thought he gave us a chance,” Brown said. “He came in, threw the ball downfield well. I don’t think we had any issues from an operations standpoint. He’s got a good vertical — he’s not a side-to-side runner, he’s a vertical runner. But he gave us a chance to win the game. I think what you want out of your game, you want an opportunity to win games. The moment wasn’t too big for him nor will Saturday be too big for him. The kid’s played in huge high school games. He won a 6A state championship. He’s a winner, and he did that on Saturday night with limited reps.”

In Jimerson’s most extensive action to date, he completed 7-of-12 passes for 70 yards and a touchdown. North Texas also ran some option-oriented packages with him, and he managed a team-high 33 rushing yards and a touchdown in the defeat. Brown had to make adjustments to the offense on the fly against the Roadrunners, relying on concepts the sophomore had practiced most frequently. Now, with a full week of preparation ahead of his first start, the coaching staff can build a more tailored game plan.

“He didn’t play a lot last year,” Brown said. “He got some good reps in spring, lot of good reps in fall camp. He was the backup for the last month, so his reps increased. The more reps he’s gotten, his decision making and confidence have grown. I thought on Saturday, when Tayven got hurt and I had a chance to collect my thoughts, my immediate thoughts on that game were, ‘What do we have a bunch of reps in? What did we rep this week in practice? What have we logged reps in where we can go out and he can operate?’ Now, all our gameplanning on Sunday and Monday was with him in mind. So now, he’s got all these reps on Monday through Friday, and the gameplan is specifically for him.”

As early as Sunday, Brown already started devising his first gameplan with Jimerson. The first-year North Texas head coach had the quarterback write down his favorite plays, explain why he preferred those plays, and then the two determined evaluated how those concepts would stack up against the Tulane defense, which they face next Saturday.

Despite the quarterback change, North Texas doesn’t expect a dramatic overhaul of its offensive identity. The Mean Green enter their upcoming matchup ranked fourth nationally in scoring at 41.8 points per game and fifth in offensive production at 512 yards per game. Many elements from that dynamic offense will still be on full display as Jackson hands the keys at quarterback to Jimerson.

“When people turn it on or our fans watch it, they’re not gonna see any difference in what our offense looks like with Tayven in at quarterback compared to Chris,” Brown said. “But there’s some intricacies where Tayven likes certain plays and where Chris likes certain plays. We’re still gonna be a balanced team. We’ve been a balanced team, 50-50 all year, and we’re still gonna be a balanced team. The offense from a player’s perspective is gonna be the same, but it’s gonna be more geared to what he likes.”

Brown sees similarities between Jimerson and quarterbacks he coached during previous stops at West Virginia and Troy. He compared Jimerson’s mobility to that of former West Virginia quarterback Garrett Greene, who now plays wide receiver for the Tampa Bay Buccaneers. Brown also praised the 5’10”, 176-pound quarterback’s arm talent, an attribute he has full faith in going forward.

“Chris spins the ball as well as anybody I’ve coached, an I’ve coached some great quarterbacks — NFL quarterbacks — and his arm talent is on par,” Brown said. “He can really spin the ball. My last quarterback at Troy was very similar to Chris, so we have experience, we have some things to fall back on.”

Jimerson will make his first collegiate start Saturday, Nov. 21 in a road contest at reigning American champion Tulane. The matchup carries added significance for the sophomore, who was part of the 2025 North Texas roster that lost to the Green Wave in the conference championship. With a victory in New Orleans, Jimerson can keep the Mean Green’s American title hopes alive as they search for their first conference championship since 2004.

“He’ll be fully prepared for Saturday morning’s game, and I’m looking forward to watching him,” Brown said. “I’m excited for our football team, but I’m really excited for him.”

# Tulane upsets North Texas 28-24, while Mean Green turn to WR at emergency quarterback

North Texas turns to WR James Tyre at emergency QB, while Trace Johnson leads a late double-digit Tulane comeback.

by Steve Helwick

Nov 23, 2026, 12:47 AM UTC

351 days earlier, Tulane and North Texas collided at Yulman Stadium for an American Conference Championship Game unlike any other. For the first time in the event’s history, College Football Playoff stakes were on the line for both teams alike. Tulane defended its home turf, capturing its second championship in four years and securing a coveted CFP berth.

On Saturday, the same teams met at the same venue, albeit with vastly different stakes. North Texas entered in a three-way tie atop the American standings, potentially on track for a second consecutive appearance in the conference championship game. Meanwhile, although Tulane hadn’t enjoyed its ideal season, the Green Wave stood one win away from securing bowl eligibility for the fifth consecutive year.

Different rosters. Different head coaches. Same result.

Tulane sidetracked North Texas’ conference championship hopes in a 28-24 upset in New Orleans, as the Green Wave won back-to-back games for the first time under head coach Will Hall, while the Mean Green suffered consecutive victories for the first time under head coach Neal Brown.

North Texas took a 24-14 lead with 7:48 remaining, appearing on track to win its 14th conference game in it last 16 tries. However, the momentum quickly shifted when TJ Smith returned the ensuing kickoff 61 yards, positioning Tulane 30 yards away from the end zone. After absorbing a sack, Green Wave quarterback Trace Johnson found Destyn Hill on a post, and Hill navigated through a sea of white jerseys en route to a 37-yard touchdown.

After the defense forced a quick stop, Johnson went right back to work. He amassed 87 yards in four completions, finishing his best drive of his true freshman season with back-to-back deep balls to tight end Cameron Roberts. The first traveled 36 yards and the second went 28 yards into the end zone, where Roberts came down with a contested catch to provide the Green Wave their first lead of the afternoon, 28-24, with 2:13 remaining.

It was the best performance of Johnson’s young career, finishing 17-of-21 for 283 yards, throwing as many touchdowns (4) as incompletions in the win.

North Texas watched a 10-point advantage evaporate in under six minutes, but the Mean Green weren’t done yet. Still, snatching the lead back would be an uphill battle given the team’s depleted quarterback depth. Last Saturday, starter Tayven Jackson — who entered the week sixth in the FBS in passing yards — suffered an upper body injury that will sideline him for the remainder of the season. Backup Chaston Ditta had also been unavailable since October, leaving sophomore Chris Jimerson Jr. to make his first career start.

Things initially moved smoothly under Jimerson, who guided the Mean Green to a 14-0 lead after two possessions. However, he exited the game with roughly 12 minutes remaining in the third quarter due to injury. Down its top three quarterbacks, North Texas elected to insert 5’8”, 171 pound wide receiver James Tyre into the game at quarterback. A former quarterback at Lambert High School (GA) in 2022, Tyre threw for 2,064 yards and 21 touchdowns as a high school senior, while rushing 441 yards and eight touchdowns.

Tyre already led a 16-play, 89-yard touchdown drive earlier in the quarter, one in which North Texas did not attempt a single pass. Trailing by four in the waning minutes, the emergency quarterback guided his team deep into Tulane territory, setting up a 1st-and-10 from the 23-yard line with 25 seconds remaining. A sack and penalty pushed North Texas backward, but Tyre subsequently completed an 13-yard pass to Dalton Carnes down to the 18-yard line. As the Mean Green hurried to the line of scrimmage for their final play, Tulane defensive tackle Ed Smith IV suffered an injury, freezing the game clock at five seconds.

NEW ORLEANS, LOUISIANA - NOVEMBER 21: Cameron Roberts #87 of the Tulane Green Wave catches a pass and is tackled during fourth quarter of the game against North Texas Mean Green at Yulman Stadium on November 21, 2026 in New Orleans, Louisiana. (Photo by Morgan Simmons/Getty Images)

That allowed North Texas one final shot at the win from 18 yards out. Tyre launched a pass toward the back left corner of the end zone, but TJ Smith — who sparked the comeback with his kick return — helped complete the comeback with a pivotal deflection. Cornerback E’zaiah Shine secured the pigskin off the ricochet, preserving the Green Wave’s upset victory.

North Texas (7-4, 5-2 American) remains alive for the conference championship race despite dropping two straight games, since Memphis also fell victim to a road upset just minutes earlier. However, the result also opened the door for East Carolina, which joins North Texas and Memphis at second place in a three-way tie. The Mean Green close their regular season at home against UAB. A victory would keep their championship hopes alive, but computer rankings will ultimately determine their fate if they finish tied with Memphis and/or ECU.

Meanwhile, Tulane (6-5, 4-3 American) secured its fifth consecutive bowl appearance for the first time in program history after improving to 5-0 against North Texas all-time. The Green Wave once again prevailed in a close finish, improving to 5-1 in one-score games under Hall. They can secure a winning season next week with a victory in their regular-season finale at South Florida.
