NEW ORLEANS — The UNT football team's string of bad voodoo in New Orleans looked to be lifted early in Saturday's game at Tulane after the Mean Green jumped out to a quick 14-point lead near the end of the first quarter. Instead, it only got worse. The Mean Green saw the big lead evaporate, their young, exciting backup quarterback leave with an injury a week after starter Tayven Jackson was injured at UTSA, and left Yulman Stadium with a 28-24 loss after having to run a makeshift offense for almost the entire second half. UNT (7-4, 5-2 American) ran the ball 29 straight times after Jimerson left the game less than three minutes into the second half after seldom-used wide receiver James Tyre was thrust in at quarterback. The Mean Green appeared to be in line for a victory after a 16-play, 89-yard scoring drive that chewed up 9:02 of game action put UNT up 10 points with under eight minutes to play against a Tulane team whose offense had struggled for most of the day. The ensuing kickoff was returned to the UNT 30-yard line, and two plays later, Tulane (6-5, 4-3) was in the end zone. The Green Wave's next possession gave them the lead after needing just four plays to cover 87 yards. "You have to go through things," said head coach Neal Brown. "It's unfortunate. We didn't lose the game on offense. We lost it because we couldn't cover kicks and we couldn't cover them. The offense was a struggle, but we found a way. But we didn't play well in the other phases and that's what's so disappointing about this." Senior running back Jahiem White had 28 carries for 148 yards and added 44 yards receiving on three catches, and sophomore running back Brendon Haygood had 17 carries for 103 yards and a pair of touchdowns, as UNT went to a form of a Wildcat offense for the second half with both backs taking several direct snaps. Brown said he was proud of the way the team adjusted and fought adversity offensively, but he knows improvements will have to be made elsewhere to win next week's regular-season finale at home against UAB, which is set for Saturday at 3 p.m. "I'm proud of how they fought and how they computed," he said. "I'm gutted for them. But at the same time, fundamentally we're not good enough. We lost because we didn't do the fundamental things on defense and special teams." QUICK HITS

UNT FB Game Notes 2026 11 Tulane

DENTON – The UNT football team returns to action on Saturday, as the Mean Green will travel to New Orleans to take on Tulane with kickoff set for 11 a.m. The game will be televised on ESPNU, with the radio broadcast set for 1190 AM/The Varsity Network. Head coach Neal Brown and select players spoke to the media on Tuesday and discussed, among other things, not focusing on the American Conference championship race, some positives to take from the UTSA game for the defense, a new quarterback under center for the Mean Green and a career year in the works for defensive lineman Davion Rhodes. UNT still in title game hunt but team focused solely on this week With two regular-season games remaining, the Mean Green are still very much in the hunt for a chance to play for the American Conference championship. Following Saturday night's loss at UTSA, UNT is now in a three-way tie atop the conference standings with UTSA and Memphis and would need to win out while also having Memphis or UTSA drop one of their remaining two games. Head coach Neal Brown has been a part of several teams in the conference championship running throughout his career at Troy and West Virginia and always had the same mindset. "We have to find a way to win this week," Brown said. "We're not worried about the conference race. We just have to figure out a way to win this game. I still believe in our team and I think we're as good or better as any team in our league, now we have to go prove it." His attitude toward worrying about the rest of the season as a whole has rubbed off on his players, just ask defensive lineman David Onuoha. "We can't focus on that because it ultimately doesn't matter," he said. "If we don't beat Tulane this week then it doesn't matter, so the focus is only on beating Tulane." Defense has positives to take from UTSA loss UNT trailed UTSA on Saturday night 21-3 at halftime after the Mean Green offense struggled to move the ball and the defense allowed 191 yards of total offense. But in the second half, UNT's defense flipped a switch and allowed just 30 yards of offense while the offense was able to make up some ground and cut the lead to 21-16 in the fourth quarter. It came unraveled a bit late in the game, as UTSA scored two late touchdowns to put the game out of reach, but Brown said the defense has to build on what it did in the second half up to that point. "Defensively, that was our best performance," Brown said. "Especially in the second half until those last two drives, we played well. We've been getting better on defense. I was disappointed in the last drive before half and the two big runs at the end but other than that we played well." That defensive performance is one of the main reasons Brown said he knows his team will play well on Saturday after taking their first loss since September 19. "You're rolling and won six in a row and all the sudden you have a loss," Brown said. "We still have a ton to play for. Offensively, we should be embarrassed how we played. We should be really hungry to get back out there and play. Defensively, we have to take confidence out of that game. We'll play really well at Tulane, whether it'll be good enough, we'll find out, but we're going ot play well." Jimerson getting his shot under center The last time sophomore quarterback Chris Jimerson Jr. started a football game, it was in December 2024, in front of more than 36,000 people at AT&T Stadium in Arlington. That day, he led his North Crowley team to 50-21 win over Austin Westlake to close his high school career with a Class 6A Division I state championship. Now, he'll be back in the starting lineup on Saturday for the Mean Green in place of star quarterback Tayven Jackson, who will miss the game with an injury he suffered in the loss to UTSA. Jimerson gave UNT a chance to win Saturday night's game against the Roadrunners with limited practice reps, but now he's getting first-team reps and will be fully prepared for whatever challenges Tulane presents. "What do you want from your quarterback? A chance to win games, and he gave us plenty of chances to win a game," Brown said. "He's played in huge games and won a 6A state championship in high school, and now he's getting a week of reps. He'll be prepared. He was prepared for limited action on Saturday night and he handled it pretty well when he was thrown in there. He'll be fully prepared for Saturday morning's game. I'm looking forward to wathing him. I'm excited for him... "Chris spins the ball as well as anyone I've ever coached, and I've coached some great quarterbacks – NFL quarterbacks. His arm talent is off the charts. He can spin the ball." Brown added that Jimerson has had the chance to sit behind some elite talent — both in how they play on the field and how they prepare off it. "I wasn't here with Drew (Mestemaker), but from I understand, he prepared at a high level," Brown said. "I know Tayven prepared at a high level. So he's been around that for two years. I'm not worried about Saturday. I'm concerned with making sure he's doing the right things as we go through the week. But he's a worker. He loves football and has no problem putting in the work through the week. If he handles that, he'll perform well." One of the players who will be blocking for Jimerson said he has ultimate confidence in the sophomore signal-caller. "He's gonna be great for us," said fifth-year right tackle Johnny Williams IV. "He's an electric guy. He does everything you want in an athlete. He'll give us a chance to win with his abilty to run and throw. He's an all-around athlete. He can do anything." Rhodes in midst of career year Fifth-year defensive end Davion Rhodes has enjoyed his one year with the Mean Green. After spending his previous years at Georgia Southern, Rhodes is currently in the middle of a career season with career-highs in both tackles for-loss (9.0) and sacks (6.5) and counting. Onuoha said Rhodes' mindset and approach is a big reason why he's excelling this season. "He's a dog," Onuoha said. "He's one of the older players on the team and he's steady. He doesn't change with a win or a loss, he's just a consistent guy and that is paying off for him. This is his best year in terms of pass rush in his college carer, and I'm happy for him and the success he's having."
