# Vikings at Packers Week 10 Game Preview

 Nov 13, 2026 at 04:14 PM

![Author Image](https://static.clubs.nfl.com/image/upload/t_thumb_squared/t_lazy/f_png/vikings/gigsl6mqfbltgplbjbdy.png)

Rob Kleifield

Writer, Editor & Quality Control

EAGAN, Minn. — This week has been a whirlwind.

The Vikings offense stumbled Monday night. On Tuesday, the team traded for 38-year-old Kirk Cousins, who started 88 regular-season games for Minnesota from 2018-23. He met with media members on Wednesday and talked, among other things, about feeling like his whole career has been "unfinished business."

The affable quarterback took the field for the first time Thursday in his reclaimed No. 8 jersey.

Goosebumps — not because it was 35° — followed. The sun peeked through a layer of clouds, and Creed's "Higher" blared from the speakers, a new (or old) addition to the playlist. Vikings players who have joined the team since 2024 learned how Cousins and the locker room previously enjoyed that song as a rallying force.

It felt like this team regained some pizzazz, even after losing consecutive games following a 6-0 start. Now, Minnesota will try to get back on track in an important divisional clash at Green Bay (6-3) with Cousins at quarterback, but will do it without starters Blake Cashman (concussion protocol) and T.J. Hockenson (chest/wrist), as well as Tyrion Ingram-Dawkins (ankle).

That trio has been ruled out, in addition to Kyler Murray (hamstring) and Jauan Jennings (calf), who will miss their second straight games. Three others are questionable: Kyle Van Noy (calf), and Jordan Addison (thumb) and Joshua Metellus (shoulder), who were added to the injury report Friday.

Let's preview the 133 rd Border Battle between the Vikings and the Packers.

3 Prominent Topics

1. Time is a flat circle

Truly, the script writers couldn't outdo the circumstances and opportunity in front of Cousins and the Vikings. He has the chance to be the first quarterback in NFL history to play 18 regular-season games. He can essentially fill in the back half of 2023, which was robbed from him when he tore his Achilles at Lambeau Field in Week 8, and he can make the Purple a three-phase contender — and he can furnish his greatest statistical season yet — with good performances in the final nine games.

Since leaving Minnesota in 2024 free agency, Cousins has appeared in 33 games for Atlanta (24) and Las Vegas (nine). He's thrown for 7,569 yards at a 64.7% clip with 48 touchdowns and 29 interceptions, good for a 90.2 passer rating, while the QB collective of Sam Darnold, J.J. McCarthy, Carson Wentz, Max Brosmer and Kyler Murray have combined to pass for 8,163 yards at a 63.6% clip with 61 TDs and 38 INTs for an 88.5 rating over 42 games in that span. Darnold propped up that quintet with 4,319 yards and 35 TDs in 17 contests two years ago. Suffice to say, the post-2024 Vikings passing game has struggled to find a rhythm; Minnesota ranks 29 th in completion rate (61.4%), 30 th in TDs with as many picks (26), and ranks 32 nd in yards (4,120) and passer rating (77.5).

That his second Vikings stint begins where his first ended is neat at the least. Cousins is 6-4-1 in his career vs. Green Bay and 2-2-1 at Lambeau Field, with a 93.8 passer rating and a 9:6 TD-to-INT split.

2. Dynamic defense

Brian Flores' bunch is enjoying a torrid start. Here's where they rank in a handful of categories through a team's first eight games since 2020: The unit has surrendered 116 points, which is the second fewest to the 2024 Chargers (101) — and the fewest permitted over the first eight by the Purple since allowing 114 in 1986; they also are No. 2 in opponent passer rating with a mark of 67.9 (Buffalo charged QBs with a 62.9 rating in 2021); and they have allowed five passing touchdowns to match the 2021 Bills and 2024 Bears for the second fewest, as well (the 2022 Broncos gave up four).

For the most part, Minnesota has put a stranglehold on rushing attacks, too. Although players rebuked the idea that a near 43-to-17-minute difference in time of possession on Monday Night Football affected them physically, being on the field for that long is not a winning formula; they faced a similar story in Week 8 at Detroit when the TOP was about 36 to 24. Taking mind of those gaps, here's how the Vikings run defense has performed in 30 of 32 quarters this season (excluding the final stanzas vs. the Lions and the Bills): they've allowed 4.1 yards a pop and four touchdowns.

On a related note, opponents have scored a TD on only 26.3% of red-zone excursions this season; that would be the stingiest single-season mark by any team since at least 2000 per NFL Research.

3. Eyes on Love

Jordan Love is the NFL passing yards leader entering Week 10 — he's on pace for a Packers franchise record 4,945 — and he gutted New England's secondary last Sunday, finishing 23-for-29 with 372 yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions. It was his most yards without a turnover and his third-most yards in a game in his career. For what it's worth, his Top 2 performances by yardage were against — you guessed it — Minnesota in Week 4 in 2024 (389) and in Week 1 this season (387). But in contrast to the Pats game, which featured a career-best 153.2 passer rating, Love didn't exactly thrive in those rivalries; his rating was 88.1 in the opener and 83.0 in that 2024 matchup to rank 37 th and tied for 39 th out of 57 starts. Impressively, Love has a rating above 100 in nearly half of them (27 of 57), but in six against the Vikings — and Flo's creative schematics — he's eclipsed the century mark just once, and it was in the penultimate game of the 2023 slate. Love's top passer rating in the five other meetings against Flores is 91.7 set in Week 17 two years ago.

The Packers QB is doing many things well, and these two exceptionally: getting the ball out quickly and threading explosives. Love's time to throw is 2.60 seconds, according to Next Gen Stats, which is second to Aaron Rodgers last season (2.59) for the quickest among qualifying QBs since 2025 — Flo's forces have the third-fastest time to pressure in 2026 at 2.55 seconds, by the way. Love also is making defenses pay in chunks. He's attempted 54 passes traveling 20-plus air yards (13 more than anyone else), and he has 40 completions gaining 20-plus yards (eight more than the second player).

Love has league-leading production against the blitz (967 yards, 10 TDs), but he's not immune to it. He was picked off once, sacked four times and pressured on 19 of 35 drop backs when the Vikings sent an extra rusher(s) his direction Sept. 13. Minnesota has blitzed on 72.5% of drop backs overall.

A tackling machine, Williams is tied for the most tackles among NFL defensive backs (76) entering Week 10 and boasts 23 more than Green Bay's No. 2 tackler, LB Isaiah McDuffie. On occasion, tackles can be a half-baked stat; they don't say whether a tackle was made 1 or 50 yards beyond the LOS. But in Williams' case they underline his ability to make an impact on every blade of grass.

Williams primarily factors as a free safety and is elite in coverage, but he's aligned in the box on approximately 100 snaps this year, as well. He's physical, and a proficient tackler (only four whiffs and a low 5.0% missed tackle rate). He's also instinctive and a ball-hawker in run and pass phases.

In coverage, Williams has given up 13 catches for 84 yards and a touchdown on 24 targets. He has intercepted three passes, and his passer rating allowed is 36.1, which ranks second among qualifying safeties per Next Gen Stats (Jevon Holland, 11.7).
