# Quarterback Kirk Cousins Starting Vikings at Packers Week 10 Game

 Nov 13, 2026 at 03:11 PM

![Author Image](https://static.clubs.nfl.com/image/upload/t_thumb_squared/t_lazy/f_png/vikings/vyfc2xmeq4vuysp9wuqn.png)

EAGAN, Minn. — Justin Jefferson, Jordan Addison, Tai Felton and Myles Price were playing a video game during Tuesday's off day for Vikings players when news broke that Kirk Cousins would be rejoining the team.

"Jets was like, 'It's solidified. We got Kirk,' " Addison explained this week a few lockers down from the one that now has Cousins' No. 8 jersey. "I was like, 'Wow, Kirko Chainz is really back. It's like 2023 all over again.' "

Cousins will start, Vikings Head Coach Kevin O'Connell announced Friday, when the Vikings (6-2) make their annual trip to Lambeau Field to face the Packers (6-3).

It will be the same venue where he made his 88 th start as a Viking in 2023 and played well (23-of-31 passing for 274 yards and two scores) before suffering a season-ending Achilles injury. The highs of a road division win and getting to 4-4 on the season after a fumble-ridden start to 2023 were quelled. A chain reaction that has involved eight players starting at QB in the three years since followed.

Familiarity with the coaching staff and multiple teammates have helped Cousins reacclimate to the Vikings locker room this week.

"I cannot give him enough credit for coming in here, how he's going about the week, both him and the coaching staff, getting him ready to go, working extremely hard, using all the time we possibly can," O'Connell said. "It's been really impressive what he's been able to do and just the command and just getting right back in the huddle with a lot of guys he's played with, but certainly some guys he hasn't. And it's been something that everybody's been very, very impressed by."

Addison sizzled in his time with Cousins — he caught 36 passes for 482 yards and seven touchdowns in his first eight pro games — but the sample size was shortened when Cousins suffered the injury in Week 8 of 2023 at Lambeau Field.

"I was super excited to get a guy of his caliber, and he's familiar with the offense. He's been around these weapons. Hopefully we get back on the same page, right where we left off," said Addison, who was then asked to summarize Thursday's practice and said, "It felt like he never left. The chemistry is still there."

Addison was added to the injury report Friday as a limited participant and is listed as questionable because of a thumb injury. O'Connell said Addison and Joshua Metellus (shoulder) were "a little banged up" during Friday's practice.

Jefferson, who launched his career at a prolific pace by snagging passes from Cousins from 2020-23, said "it's definitely exciting to have a guy like him back in the building."

"We know how he approaches the game, how he studies, how he is mentally and physically preparing for Sunday, so it's just a joy to hear him just take over and be a leader in the meeting room or on the field," Jefferson said. "It's definitely a cool little situation."

Jefferson is entering Week 10 with 31 catches for 393 yards and four touchdowns. His 56.1 yards per game is well below the lofty 90.2 he averaged in his historic first six seasons. The star wants to boost his production — and more importantly help Minnesota return to the win column after two consecutive losses — but he also has seen Cousins not over-force things if reading the coverage takes the QB to an open teammate.

"I think he just understands the game. He's a guy that studies the game a lot. He understands how the defenses play, especially covering me," Jefferson said. "And he understands, just like he said the other day, the plays that we have called that are kind of my plays, and he understands that the timing and the windows to throw those type of plays, but he also doesn't force those type of plays to me and he just lets the game come to him."

Cousins wound up playing nine games for the Raiders, where he reunited with former Vikings Offensive Coordinator Klint Kubiak. He completed 202 of 315 passes for 2,340 yards with 20 touchdowns against eight interceptions and a passer rating of 97.1.

He's also continued climbing the NFL leaderboard, passing Vikings Ring of Honor and Pro Football Hall of Fame QB Fran Tarkenton (47,003) for 15 th in career passing yardage (47,040) last week.

"He's accurate, he's a great decision maker. He understands the game at a very, very high level, understands the passing game in the NFL and the rhythm and timing that separates plays from being successful or not," O'Connell said. "And then I think on top of it, just the way he's gone about it, the way you feel a command.

"It's one thing to have command and ownership over plays in a system," O'Connell continued. "And I think we talk a lot about that, but it's more so about his job and his job impacting in a really positive way the other 10 guys in the huddle doing theirs, how he goes about it, the poise, the command of the huddle, the ownership of cadence, the ownership of the small details that he works really hard at to make sure that he can do those things because he knows how important it is."

Although the practice week has wrapped, the coaching staff and QB planned additional time to continue going over things, using "every moment we have up until the game."

"But I'm really looking forward to seeing him play," O'Connell said. "It's something we've always known about him, but the way he threw the football this week just was — it's a really cool thing to see."

Drew Lock ‘Confident And Optimistic’ Heading Into Sunday’s Game & Other Seahawks Updates

# Drew Lock 'Confident And Optimistic' Heading Into Sunday's Game & Other Seahawks Updates

## Injury updates from Seahawks coach Mike Macdonald heading into Sunday’s game against the Raiders.

 Nov 13, 2026 at 02:56 PM

![Author Image](https://static.clubs.nfl.com/image/private/t_thumb_squared/t_lazy/f_auto/seahawks/ml716a6zjck2uisnmabb.jpg)

John Boyle

Seahawks.com Senior Reporter

![20261111_PRACTICE_RM1_4145](https://static.clubs.nfl.com/image/upload/t_editorial_landscape_mobile/t_lazy/f_auto/seahawks/xlhxrffbvxvjusetdcsq.jpg)

SEATTLE SEAHAWKS

For the second straight week, Drew Lock was limited in practice as he prepares to start in place of an injured Sam Darnold, but despite the oblique and elbow injuries that have limited Lock's reps in practice, he and the team are confident in his preparation heading into Seattle's Week 10 game in Las Vegas.

"He had a similar cadence and workload in terms of last week," Seahawks coach Mike Macdonald said following Friday's practice. "It's good to have done it and gotten through the week well. I know he's confident and optimistic, so we're in good shape."

Macdonald also made a point to praise the work that second-year quarterback Jalen Milroe has done in practice these past two weeks to be ready to play in case Lock were to have a setback.

"I think it's appropriate to say Jalen has done a tremendous job when he's been in there with the ones," Macdonald said. "I've been impressed. He's worked extremely hard, he's been decisive, he's delivered the ball accurately, on time. He's done a great job filling in for Drew (Lock) when we need him."

A day earlier, Lock said "I'm feeling good" when asked about preparing through injuries.

"Like I talked about after the game, we did a great job handling the week last week, and here we are," he said. "We just had a little something added to it, but I think we're doing the same. We're doing a great job getting ready, good stuff in the morning before meetings and be nice and warmed up heading into practice today and we'll go out there and roll it."

In more positive injury news, the Seahawks are getting back three players who were out the last two weeks due to injury: outside linebacker Uchenna Nwosu, receiver Rashid Shaheed and nose tackle Brandon Pili. All three were full participants in Friday's practice and do not have a game status, meaning they are good to go.

In addition to Darnold being out again with quadricep and rib injuries, the Seahawks also ruled out guard Quenton Nelson (knee), tackle Charles Cross (ankle) and defensive tackle Leonard Williams (stinger).

Of that group, only Cross practiced during the week as a limited participant on Thursday, and Macdonald said Cross is on track to return when the Seahawks face the 49ers after their Week 11 bye.

"I think it's fair to say he should be ready for the game after the bye," Macdonald said. "He's done a great job working through all the things, he couldn't make this game, unfortunately."

Asked about Darnold's injury, Macdonald said, "I see Sam progressing with his rehab and the guys are saying he's doing a great job and he's progressing. We'll take it a day and a week at a time."

As for Williams, who is trying to get back from a stinger, Macdonald said, "It's one of those things where it's not good until it is. It's not good yet… He's able to move around and do all the things. Stingers are more of a contact thing. He's doing a good job staying in shape and staying fresh. He's in the meetings, he's doing all the Leo things. He's just not playing right now."

On a non-injury note, defensive tackle Jarran Reed was back with the team for Friday's practice after being out the previous two days with what was listed as a personal matter on the injury report. Macdonald said Reed will play Sunday and referred to it as a "good personal issue" that kept him away for a couple of days.

# For Starters, Carson Beck Ready To Play First Game Against Rams

## Rookie takes over at QB after Minshew's season-ending injury

 Nov 11, 2026 at 05:44 PM

![Author Image](https://static.clubs.nfl.com/image/upload/t_thumb_squared/t_lazy/f_auto/cardinals/r10hui6vkhnq462o98lg.jpg)

Darren Urban

azcardinals.com

![Rookie quarterback Carson Beck will make his first career start at State Farm Stadium on Sunday against the Rams.](https://static.clubs.nfl.com/image/upload/t_editorial_landscape_mobile/t_lazy/f_auto/cardinals/sz3tncitabokpmrrpgpa.jpg)

Jeremy Chen/Arizona Cardinals

Rookie quarterback Carson Beck will make his first career start at State Farm Stadium on Sunday against the Rams.

After Carson Beck got the call he was going to be starting at quarterback for the Cardinals, he couldn't stop happily pacing his backyard, calling family and friends to deliver the news.

"Obviously, this is a dream of mine," the rookie said on Wednesday. "I was ready to play right then and there."

That Beck, the Cardinals' third-round draft pick, was going to play this season was nearly inevitable from the start, his electric debut in the Hall of Fame game back on Aug. 6 short-circuited quickly by a ribs injury that delayed his timeline.

That timeline was also delayed behind two veteran quarterbacks. But in the span of two weeks, Jacoby Brissett lost his job (and was traded) and Gardner Minshew wrecked his elbow and was lost for the season.

The Cardinals had no choice but to go Beck to the future.

"It's been a crazy season, it's really been a welcome-to-the-NFL season, being introduced to what the league can be," Beck said.

If Minshew hadn't gotten hurt, Beck would still be on the bench. (Although for how much longer is unknown and also moot.) Beck will get an eight-game runway to show the Cardinals what he can bring, driving an analysis of what the organization will need to do in the offseason when it comes to quarterback.

Beck, who turns 25 later this month, has been in the spotlight his whole college career. He played in nationally televised college playoff games with Georgia and Miami, his last collegiate victory in the national semifinals at State Farm Stadium and the Fiesta Bowl.

But given that his last snap in a game was back in Canton, Ohio more than three months ago, there is an unknown.

"You never really know until you get to the live bullets on Sunday, but just the way he goes about his business … I have all the confidence that he's ready to roll," coach Mike LaFleur said.

Sitting and learning wasn't always what the fan base clamored for with Beck, but it was the plan by the Cardinals. With as much NFL football Brissett and Minshew have played, that rubbed off on Beck as he sat and watched.

"Everybody is looking at you as a quarterback, right?" Beck said. "Your attitude has to be right each and every day, the moxie, going out and having confidence, making everyone around you better, I think that's something I learned."

Whether it was being under center for two national powers or just his natural makeup, Beck said it's important to show a swag or moxie as a QB, not only for himself but to bring others that vibe, regardless of what is going on at the time.

His work as a backup was to take all the mental reps, which given his injuries (ribs in preseason, back spasms recently), was the best thing to do. Besides, he said, he wasn't getting starter-like reps anyway and mental reps always are available.

The circumstances aren't perfect. The first defense he'll face features two of the best pass rushers of this generation, Myles Garrett and Aaron Donald. His wide receiver corps looks nothing like what the Cardinals had on the field in Week 1.

But he still has All-Pro tight end Trey McBride, a player with whom Beck has built a friendship with off the field and one that marvels at Beck's smarts and ability to quickly get through his reads on the field.

"I knew his time was coming," McBride said.

Beck referenced a video clip LaFleur showed the team this season. It featured basketball star Steph Curry recalling his big shots against France in the 2024 gold medal basketball game.

"You can't be afraid of a missed shot or a bad possession because that will kind of handcuff you," Curry said in the clip. "You have to be comfortable with the unpredictability of the outcome."

That resonates with Beck as he takes the field in the pros for the first time against a team that has lost once already to the Cardinals.

"That's how you handle the emotions of it," Beck said. "You want to go out and do your best. But when you think too much about that, when you fear failure or you fear not getting the result you want, that's when emotions get in the way."

Beck isn't going to let that happen.

"I think the coolest thing about confidence," he said, "is that confidence is a choice."

# News & Notes: Jesse Minter Gives Injury Update on Kyle Hamilton

 Nov 09, 2026 at 05:21 PM

![Author Image](https://static.clubs.nfl.com/image/private/t_thumb_squared/t_lazy/f_auto/ravens/mqn7n8asv5kr1ddvauku.jpg)

Clifton Brown

BaltimoreRavens.com Staff Writer

![S Kyle Hamilton](https://static.clubs.nfl.com/image/upload/t_editorial_landscape_mobile/t_lazy/f_png/ravens/axpwtvr6f3jexihr9vgy.png)

Joey Pulone/Baltimore Ravens Photos

S Kyle Hamilton

Head Coach Jesse Minter gave a positive update Monday on All-Pro safety Kyle Hamilton, who left Thursday night's game against the Jacksonville Jaguars with a hip injury.

"I think everything looks fairly good," Minter said. "We'll find out as the guys come back in tomorrow (Tuesday). But I wouldn't expect that to be something where … hopefully he doesn't miss time."

Minter was also optimistic about cornerback Nate Wiggins (illness) and safety Jaylinn Hawkins (knee) returning to action this week. Wiggins did not play against the Jaguars, while Hawkins has missed the last three games.

"There's definitely optimism there," Minter said regarding Hawkins rejoining practice. "I think (Nate) will be okay. It was an unfortunate timing of an illness."

### Minter Expects Jacoby Brissett to Start Immediately and Bring Leadership

Veteran quarterback Jacoby Brissett was at the Under Armour Performance Center for much of the weekend after being acquired in a trade with the Arizona Cardinals.

In his 11 th NFL season, Brissett has the experience to transition smoothly into a starting role immediately. Brissett and Quarterbacks Coach Israel Woolfork worked together with the Cardinals last season, which will help the new quarterback get acclimated.

Minter confirmed that the Ravens expect Brissett to be their starter Monday night against the Los Angeles Chargers.

"It's an 11-year vet, he's played for a bunch of different teams, been in a bunch of different systems. I think he'll operate our offense at a level required to be successful," Minter said.

"He'll get the ball out on time. He'll be able to run the whole thing. He'll be a leader. He'll hold guys accountable. I'm just excited about the addition, the chance that gives us to kind of spark this thing."

In other quarterback commentary, Minter praised undrafted rookie quarterback Joe Fagnano for the way he handled his first career start against the Jaguars. Fagnano, who completed 10 of 18 passes for 60 yards and threw two interceptions, played on a heavily wrapped ankle after being injured four days earlier against the Buffalo Bills. Having a short week to bounce back before a Thursday night game, Fagnano showed the poise and toughness that helped him make the 53-man roster as a long shot.

"That guy earned a lot of respect in the locker room for going out there on Thursday night and dealing with what he was dealing with physically and playing and gutting through it and giving us everything that he had," Minter said.

"He's well aware of how we feel about him. I think he gets a chance now, again, as another guy that can honestly benefit from a guy like Jacoby coming in. This is the type of role that he could have for a long time if he takes this opportunity and really learns from it."

### Minter Says He Will Keep Lamar Jackson Injury Updates Private

Minter said he will not be sharing regular injury updates on Lamar Jackson, who is on the injured list with a thumb injury.

"I'm going to keep most of the conversations between Lamar and I," Minter said. "Nobody wants to be on the field more. He's probably going to do everything he can to put himself in position. But I'll leave it at that."

Minter maintained his stance when asked if, or when, Jackson will have surgery.

"I'm going to keep all that stuff between me and him," Minter said. "You may know, but that'll come from him."

### Minter on Trade Deadline: 'We'll See'

The NFL trade deadline is Tuesday, and the Ravens are constantly exploring ways to improve the team. Minter said he was happy Baltimore acquired Brissett but was uncertain if more deals would follow after speaking with General Manager Eric DeCosta.

"It's kind of a 'we'll see,'" Minter said. "We made the move to get Jacoby for our team right now. That's a much-needed move, one we're very happy about from a coaching perspective. Anything that beyond that, we'll kind of see what happens."

Minter added that he is happy with the talent on Baltimore's roster.

"I like our team," Minter said. "Now, we need to play better. We need to do things better. But we have some good players that we need to coach better, we need to get them to play the way we're capable of playing."
